# Bloom Resort: video series plan

A 6-episode vertical (9:16) video series for Bloom Pour. The cat is the resort VIP, a guest in a room with a view. The estate (Ki Residences pool, stone seats, lit garden path, trees) is the view. **The cat stays at home.** The estate is filmed separately, without the cat, and cut in as what the cat sees.

**Season premise: haze season at the resort.** Severe haze has lasted for weeks, so every estate shot will be hazy. Instead of waiting for clear weather, the haze is the story: a warm room with a grey view, and a VIP cat who stays in. Every episode keeps its gag; the view is always grey, and the delivery call to action reads as "stay in, we bring the cup". See "Haze look" below.

Status: draft. Episode 1 is detailed and ready to shoot. Episodes 2-6 are outlined and need shot-level detail before filming.

## Brand hook

A pour-over "bloom" is when hot water first hits the grounds and the bed swells up. The cat's matching moment is something it does reliably at home. The match-cut carries the tagline: *every pour, a small bloom.*

Hook options, easiest first (a cat stretch is hard to catch, so it is a bonus, not the plan):
1. **Eyes opening.** A drowsy cat's eyes go from half-closed to open as the bloom swells. Main hook.
2. **Breathing belly.** A resting or sleeping cat's belly rises slowly. Backup.
3. **Ears perk up.** Ears snap toward the pour sound (play a kettle or pour sound near the cat). Backup.
4. **Round-shape match.** The bloom dome cuts to the loaf pose or the top of the cat's head. Needs no movement.
5. **Paw kneading.** Rhythm matches the bloom's pulse. Not controllable.
6. **Stretch** (bonus): film right after a nap, or hold a treat high or behind the cat's head. Or play loaf-settling footage in reverse.

All of 1-3 can come from one nap session. Shoot them together and choose in the edit.

AI option: an image-to-video model on a photo of the cat could generate a short stretch clip (fal.ai key is set in OpenMontage's `.env`). Limits: the generated cat will not match the real cat's markings, motion can look wrong, each clip costs credits, and Instagram's AI label applies. Use only if real footage fails, or for the dream sequence in Episode 4.

## Rules repeated in every episode

- **Opening (first 3s):** macro of the coffee bed blooming, hard match-cut to the cat (eyes opening).
- **Closing (last 3s):** cat settles into a loaf and closes its eyes. Same sound, same end card.
- **Cups-left counter** on screen (e.g. "12 cups left"), matching live stock from the order app.
- **Fixed "rooms":** the cat's window seat (home), the coffee station (home), and estate views (outside, no cat): pool, path, stone seats.
- **Eyeline:** the cat always looks in the same screen direction, toward the window, so estate cutaways match.
- **Haze is the look:** estate views are hazy and grey in every episode; the room stays warm. No health claims in captions (see "Haze look").
- **Private info stays private:** never show brew ratio or temperature from the coffee library (the app keeps these admin-only).

## Episodes 2-6: outline only

**2. The Review.** All at home. Cat sniffs 3-4 coffees currently on sale. Reaction becomes the tasting note as a cat-voice caption: ears forward is "fruity", slow blink is "chocolatey", head tilt is "complex". Title card per coffee with origin and roastery (no brew data).

**3. Room Service.** Follow the cup from your door, through the lift and corridor, to the pool, with the haze hanging over the estate. No cat in these shots. The corridor and lift shots (Z6, Z7, S7) are indoors, so keep the outdoor stretch short.

**4. The Cat's Dream.** Cat naps at home (close-up, ears twitching, slow breathing). The dream is a low glide through the estate (stone seats, path, pool) over a slow-motion pour (50p). The haze suits it: soft, flat, grey-gold, dreamlike. Ends with the cat waking beside a real cup. An AI-generated glide is acceptable here because the surreal look suits a dream.

**5. Haze Report.** Cat is the resort forecaster. The forecast is haze, again, and the haze picks the coffee of the day from the coffees on sale. The haze edition below is the main version. If rain, mist or a clear day arrives, film it (T7, T8) as a bonus edition. This episode is the most time-sensitive, so shoot its estate footage first while the haze lasts; it can be posted ahead of Episodes 2-4.

**6. Fully Booked.** Sale-day finale. The counter drops 12, 8, 3, 0 across the video. A paper "sold out" sign sits on the sill; cat looks out at the grey view.

### Combining the fisheye circle with rectangular shots (9:16)

**What the circle means.** The circle is the cat's eye: what the cat sees (F2, F6, F7), the cat right up at the lens (F1, F3), the peephole (F9) and the dream (Ep 4). Everything else (pours, reactions, T and Z estate shots) stays rectangular. A circle appears only when the story calls for it, so the change of shape means something.

**Layout (decided):**
- Rectangular shots fill the 1080x1920 frame.
- Circle shots use one fixed **porthole**: the circle about 1000px across, centred horizontally, centre at about y 900 (slightly above middle), the same size and place in every episode.
- **Black** above and below the circle (no blurred copy).
- **Rim ring on**: a thin ring (about 6px) in the brand sage-cream `#DEE5DE` around every circle (the same ring as on the round cup sticker, `public/brand/sticker-sheet-a4.svg`, which ties the series to the cups). It is the series' visual signature and hides the soft fisheye edge.
- **Full porthole only**: no small circle inset over a rectangular shot.
- Text bands: the cups-left counter sits in the top band in both layouts, so it never jumps; captions sit in the band below the circle, and in the same lower area on rectangular shots. Keep text out of the circle and inside the Reels safe zones (the app covers the top and bottom edges).

**Transitions (never a plain hard cut from rectangle to circle):**
1. **Round-shape match cut (main move).** End the rectangular shot on something round, framed at the porthole's exact size and position, then cut to the circle: the dripper or cup from directly above (ties to the bloom hook), the cat's eye (M5, "through the VIP's eye"), the round scratcher bed, a path-light glow.
2. **Iris.** A circular mask closes the rectangular shot down to the porthole, then cuts to the fisheye inside it; reverse it to come back out. Use when no round object is available.
3. **Circle opens.** The circle scales up until its rim leaves the frame, then dissolves to the next shot. Only for big moments: falling into the dream (Ep 4) and the final lens boop (Ep 6).

| Ep | Circle beat | In | Out |
|----|-------------|----|-----|
| 1 | F2 window view (F6 later as a second accent) | Match on the cat's eye (M5), or iris on the window (S1) | Cut to the T5 pool view, same screen direction |
| 3 | F9 peephole, the cup arrives | Rectangular corridor walk (S7), then iris down to the porthole | Cut to the cat's ears perking up (M6b) |
| 4 | Dream: F3, F5 glide, F4 canopy | Sleeping cat (M7) to the F3 circle; the circle opens into the dream | Iris closes to the rectangular cat waking beside the cup |
| 5 | F2 or the F4 courtyard (iconic tree, haze-filled opening), full porthole after the cat's forecast | Match from the cup seen from above | Match back to the cup |
| 6 | F7 empty seat early; F1 boop at the end | Match on the round seat or the cup | The nose fills the circle, the circle opens to the end card |

**Ep 4 exception:** inside the dream the circle stays in place and its contents dissolve from shot to shot; this is the one place fisheye shots run back to back.

**Keeping the two looks one series:** same grade (warm room, grey haze outside), same ambient sound bed, circle accents of 2-4s with at least 5s of rectangular shots between them (Ep 4 excepted). Do not de-fish the circle into a rectangle.

**Extra shots this needs:** an M1 variant with the cup from directly above, framed so the rim matches the porthole (the key match cut); an M5 variant with the eye centred in frame; optionally a centred rectangular shot of a path-light glow or the pavilion dome.

**Build (later):** four reusable pieces in OpenMontage: porthole layout (circle mask, rim ring, black background, text bands), iris transition, circle-open, and the round-shape match helper. Source circle: centre (952, 538), radius about 452.

## Time of day

- **Home shots (cat, pours, macro):** morning window light, matching the brand line "Slow mornings". Light from the same direction as the estate views.
- **Estate B-roll:** shoot any time of day while the haze lasts; the haze softens the light, so there is no need to wait for golden hour. Dusk is still worth it for the path lights, which glow nicely in haze. Sunrise and sunset give the dull orange sun disc (T10).
- **Background reference photos:** any light. They only feed the storyboard frames.

## Matching home footage to the estate footage

1. Blurred background (plants or a soft light behind the cat).
2. Light from the same direction and colour as the estate shots; no warm bulbs.
3. Same cushion or surface in every shot.
4. Consistent screen direction for the cat's gaze.
5. Same collar, or none.
6. One shared pool and garden ambience bed under the whole video.
7. Cut between mismatched poses with a cutaway (the pour, the cup).
8. A light colour grade to unify home and estate shots.

### Visual rules for haze

- Colour story: warm and gold inside, cool grey outside. Keep white balance fixed for each location, and do not correct one to match the other.
- Haze flattens contrast and removes the blue. Do not try to make the estate shots look clear; grade for mood, not clarity.
- Haze shows layers: treeline behind treeline fading to grey. That is what T9 is for.
- Shoot from inside, through a clean closed window, where possible. Put the lens hood against the glass or hang a dark cloth to cut reflections.
- Sound: windows closed means a quiet room. Keep a low ambient bed, not the pool sounds used in other episodes.
- Cat stays indoors with the windows closed. Keep any outdoor time for the estate shots short.
- Do not make health claims in the caption (no mention of air quality, protection or safety). Keep it about staying in and the coffee.

### Shoot now (while the haze lasts)

1. Estate shots first: T9, T10, T5, Z1, Z2, Z4. Keep outdoor time short and wear a mask if the air is unhealthy.
2. Then home shots at any time: S1, S11, M5, S2, S3, M4, S5, S8.
3. The same Z and T shots serve Episodes 1, 3, 4 and 6 too. Shoot each once, in the haze, and reuse it. Only re-shoot in clear weather if the haze lifts before the series is posted and you want a clear-sky variant.

- **Horizon through the centre.** In a fisheye, lines through the centre of the circle stay straight and lines off-centre curve. Put the waterline or horizon through the centre when you want it straight (F6) and off-centre when you want a planet look (F5).
- **No filters, no ND.** The front element bulges, so use the haze, shade, f/8 and ISO 100 in the day, and f/4 with higher ISO at dusk. Expose for the sky and lift the shadows in the grade (F2_t02 came out about 1 stop dark for this reason).
- **Sun and flare.** The sun disc is fine in live view, but keep it brief. Shade the front of the hood with a hand or a card to cut flare.
- **Wipe the front element** after every setup: haze dust, pool spray and fingerprints all show up as smudges across the whole circle.
- **People.** The circle sees 180 degrees, so check behind and beside the lens before rolling, then check the frame on playback.
- **Haze.** Do not lift the circle to look clear. Add a little contrast only if the grey flattens the shapes, so the outdoor circles sit with the F2 grade.
- **Keep outdoor time short**; wear a mask if the air is unhealthy (see "Shoot now").
- **Water.** The camera tethers to your wrist for F6 and F8; use the beanbag, not the tripod, and never lean over water to adjust it.

### Starting settings (Movie mode)

Each lens section above has a per-frame exposure table. The general rules:

- Full HD, 25p (PAL, Singapore). 50p for slow motion, slowed to 50% in the edit.
- Movie exposure: Manual on both bodies (Movie menu). Auto exposure pumps the brightness when lights or the sky enter the frame.
- Set the three in this order:
  1. **Shutter** follows the frame rate: 1/50 at 25p, 1/100 at 50p. Leave it there.
  2. **Aperture** from the frame's table: f/2.8-f/4 for the cat, f/4-f/5.6 for macro, f/8 for estate landscapes, f/4-f/5.6 at dusk.
  3. **ISO** last, to get the exposure right.
- Too bright at ISO 100: add ND (50mm, 100mm, 70-200mm). With no filter thread (Zeiss 15mm, fisheye), raise the shutter to 1/100-1/200 or stop down to f/11 instead.
- Too dark: open to the widest aperture the frame allows, then raise ISO. Limits: 6D Mark II ISO 6400, 700D ISO 3200.
- Check the histogram, not the screen; the screen lies outdoors. Haze sky: just short of the right edge. Dusk: lights may touch the right edge, the rest should not.
- White balance: manual Kelvin, fixed across a shoot (about 5600K daylight, 7000K or higher blue hour).
- Picture style: Neutral or Faithful, sharpness 0, contrast -2.
- Focus: 6D Mark II, Movie Servo AF on with touch-to-focus for the cat (tap the face); manual focus on the Zeiss. 700D fisheye: AF once, then lens switch to MF and Movie Servo AF off (it hunts mid-take otherwise).
- Audio: manual levels. Record 30s of separate ambient sound at the pool.

### ND filters

Filter thread sizes (from memory; confirm on the front ring, marked with the diameter symbol):

- Canon 70-200mm f/4L: 67mm

- f/8: ND8 (0.9, 3 stops)
- f/4: ND32 (1.5, 5 stops)
- f/2.8: ND64 (1.8, 6 stops)
Shade, overcast or golden hour: 0-2 stops. A 77mm variable ND (about 2-5 stops) covers all of these; stay below about 5 stops to avoid the X-pattern cheap variable NDs show at the strongest setting. ND8 is the most useful fixed filter.

### Camera support without a gimbal

- Tripod: Manfrotto Befree 2N1 (ball head, so pans are jerky). Check its payload rating; the camera plus the 70-200 weighs about 2kg.
- Lock-off most shots. Slow push-ins of 5s or more hide shake better than pans.
- Beanbag (a bag of rice) on a sill or stone seat for low static shots.
- Neck strap pulled taut plus the "ninja walk" (elbows in, bent knees, heel-to-toe) for walking shots.
- Slide the camera on a hardback book along a smooth floor for a low push-in.
- Turn on Movie Digital IS (crops the frame slightly), and use IS lenses when handheld.
- In the edit: stabilise (FFmpeg vidstab), slow to 80-90%, crop in 5-10%.
